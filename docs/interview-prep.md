# Product Designer interview preparation guide

A study guide for scheduling-focused Product Designer interviews. Answers are frameworks: fill in with your own words and real examples, and never claim experience you don't have.

## 1. Your story in 60 seconds
- **Past:** engineering, criminology, public administration, teaching → systems, evidence, public service, explanation.
- **Present:** designing and building products (WideKonek, ZC Smart Digital, Frendora); SportSync as a deep scheduling case study.
- **Future:** product design on a team that owns complex scheduling for everyday people.

## 2. Likely questions & how to answer

| Question | Framework / points to cover |
|---|---|
| Walk me through a project. | Use the 5-minute walkthrough script. Problem → role → key decision → trade-off → what you'd test next. |
| What's your design process? | Frame → research plan → structure (IA/flows) → design states → prototype → validate → handoff → measure. Show it's a loop. |
| How do you design for multiple user types? | Role matrix; same components answering different first questions; permissions shown, not hidden (SportSync PermissionNotice). |
| How would you make AI scheduling trustworthy? | Show understanding first; explain trade-offs; human approval; editable output; undo; label limitations. |
| Tell me about a conflict-resolution design. | Cause in plain language → who's affected → choose what moves → compare 3 alternatives → confirm with before/after + who's notified. |
| How do you keep web, iOS and Android consistent? | Shared tokens, content and IA; platform-native navigation, pickers, sheets, back behaviour. Point to the platform table. |
| How do you work with engineers? | Shared PRD and acceptance criteria early; states and edge cases listed; traceability; prototype code they can read; QA together. |
| How do you measure success? | Tie metrics to decisions: conflict rate (prevention working?), resolution rate (fixes actionable?), RSVP rate (channel friction?), AI acceptance (trust/quality). |
| Your research hasn't been run. Why? | Be direct: built the kit and the prototype to test with; next step is 5 interviews + 2 usability rounds. Explain what you'd expect to learn. |
| Biggest weakness / gap? | Limited time in a large product team. Mitigation: documentation discipline, handoff artifacts, eagerness for critique. |
| A time you changed your mind? | SportSync iterations: grouped recurring conflicts; practice planner plans the next block; performance for live validation. |
| How do you handle accessibility? | Tokens with checked contrast, keyboard patterns, focus visible, target size, labels, never color alone, reduced motion. |

## 3. Scheduling domain questions to prepare
- Round robin vs pool play; why once-per-day constraints change formats.
- Recurring event edits (this / following / all) and exceptions.
- Time zones and daylight-saving shifts for travelling teams.
- Fairness: early/late slot rotation, home/away balance, rest periods.
- Notification fatigue vs missed changes; channel escalation.

## 4. Whiteboard / design exercise approach (45 min)
1. Clarify (5): users, platform, constraints, success.
2. Map (10): key task flow and edge cases.
3. Sketch (15): 2–3 screens; show states.
4. Critique (10): risks, what to test, metrics.
5. Wrap (5): summarise decisions and open questions.

## 5. Questions to ask them
- Which scheduling problem causes the most support tickets today?
- How do designers and engineers share ownership of edge cases?
- How do you research with volunteer coaches who are short on time?
- How is AI being evaluated for quality and trust?
- What would success look like for this role in 6 months?

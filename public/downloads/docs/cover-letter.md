Dear Hiring Team,

Scheduling looks simple until one gym closes for maintenance, a volunteer coach picks up an extra shift, and forty families need to know before Saturday morning. I'm applying for the Product Designer role focused on scheduling because that moment, when a small change ripples through a whole community, is the kind of problem I most enjoy designing for.

To show how I would approach it, I designed and built **SportSync**, an independent portfolio project (not affiliated with TeamSnap) covering the full scheduling lifecycle for league administrators, coaches, parents, athletes and facility managers. It includes:

- **A working prototype** where you can create recurring events, detect ten kinds of conflicts (double-booked venues, overlapping teams, unavailable coaches, insufficient rest, closures and more), and compare ranked alternatives side by side before confirming a change.
- **An AI-assisted scheduling assistant** that turns a plain-language request into a tournament proposal, shows what it understood, explains each decision, and never publishes without human approval and undo. I've labeled it clearly as a simulated, rule-based engine rather than a live AI model.
- **Consistency across web, iOS and Android** through one token-based design system, with platform-appropriate navigation and controls documented side by side.
- **Engineering collaboration artifacts**: PRD, user stories with acceptance criteria, API and database specs, a QA plan, a traceability matrix, and automated tests.

I want to be transparent about where I am. My product design work comes from independent and founder projects: WideKonek (a networking, recruitment and freelance marketplace I designed and built on Next.js and Supabase), ZC Smart Digital (automation and web demos) and Frendora (a social platform concept). SportSync's research and usability testing are fully planned but not yet run, and the portfolio says so wherever it matters.

What I bring alongside the design work is an unusual mix: chemical engineering taught me to model systems and constraints; criminology taught me to separate evidence from assumption; a master's in public administration taught me how services succeed or fail real people; and teaching taught me to explain clearly. Together they make me careful, curious and practical.

I would love to talk about how I could help coaches, parents and administrators spend less time on schedules and more time on the game. Thank you for your consideration.

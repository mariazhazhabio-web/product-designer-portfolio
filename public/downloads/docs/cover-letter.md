Dear Hiring Team,

Scheduling looks simple until one gym closes for maintenance, a volunteer coach picks up an extra shift, and forty families need to know before Saturday morning. I'm applying for the Product Designer, Scheduling role because that moment, when a small change ripples through a whole community, is the kind of problem I most enjoy designing for.

To show how I would approach it, I designed and built **SportSync**, an independent portfolio project (not affiliated with TeamSnap) that designs the scheduling experience end to end for volunteer coaches, parents and league administrators, often on a phone. It covers the full lifecycle (creating and importing schedules, RSVP and availability, conflict detection, venue and facility constraints, recurring events, calendar sync and change notifications) and includes:

- **A working prototype** where you can create recurring events, detect ten kinds of conflicts (double-booked venues, overlapping teams, unavailable coaches, insufficient rest, closures and more), and compare ranked alternatives side by side before confirming a change.
- **An AI-assisted scheduling assistant** that turns a plain-language request into a tournament proposal, shows what it understood, explains each decision, and never publishes without human approval and undo. I've labeled it clearly as a simulated, rule-based engine rather than a live AI model.
- **Consistency across web, iOS and Android** through one token-based design system, with platform-appropriate navigation and controls documented side by side.
- **Shipping with engineering**: PRD, user stories with acceptance criteria, API and database specs, a QA plan, a traceability matrix and automated tests, so what ships matches the design.
- **Design system and success metrics**: reusable components with documented states, WCAG 2.2 AA contrast checks, and 11 proposed metrics tied to design decisions.

I also use AI in my own process, to plan research, explore ideas and build prototypes faster, and I'd be excited to learn how scheduling comes together across web, iOS, Android and TeamSnap ONE.

I want to be transparent about where I am. My product design work comes from independent and founder projects: WideKonek (a networking, recruitment and freelance marketplace I designed and built on Next.js and Supabase), ZC Smart Digital (automation and web demos) and Frendora (a social platform concept). SportSync's research and usability testing are fully planned but not yet run, and the portfolio says so wherever it matters.

Alongside that, I have worked since 2008 in administration, research and policy management for a national government agency in the Philippines, where I learned how rules, processes and real people meet in public services, and why small mistakes in a workflow matter. Earlier, I supported customers by phone, email and chat, and I have taught English online. What I bring alongside the design work is an unusual mix: chemical engineering taught me to model systems and constraints; criminology taught me to separate evidence from assumption; a master's in public administration taught me how services succeed or fail real people; and my education degree and teaching licence taught me to explain clearly. Together they make me careful, curious and practical.

I would love to talk about how I could help coaches, parents and administrators spend less time on schedules and more time on the game. I work remotely, am comfortable collaborating across time zones with overlap with US hours by agreed schedule, and am available for interviews by appointment. Thank you for your consideration.
